- **Model form:** exactly a landmark's `model.json`: `{tris, pos, col, idx}`;
  metres; y up, x east, z south; origin at the middle of the base; one RGB
  colour (0-255) per corner; no normals. The app's mesh shader already shades
  from screen slopes (`cross(dFdx, dFdy)`), and `landmarks.js buildVertices()`
  already turns this form into the app's 16-byte vertices (xyz float + RGBA8).
- **index.json:** `{ note, sets: [ { id, name, models: [ { id, name, osm, stage, status, notes, part? } ] } ] }`.
  Status: `approved`, `check` (changed since Jim last saw it), `testing` (to be
  judged in the world), `pending` (waiting on something), `rework`, `new`.
- **tags.json:** one entry per tag: `{tag, name, how, status, notes, seen, where}`.
  Status as above plus `n/a` (recommended against; the reason is in the notes).
  Today: 11 approved (the two vineyard tags now), 407 pending, 204 n/a. Shown on the library page's
  Map tags tab.
- **Test:** `node test/run.mjs library` fails if a listed object has no shape,

## 5. Trees: how they work

**Styles and stages.** Each style has one shape per growth stage, built at real
size, and one simple far shape.

| Style | Stands for | Stages (ids `style-1`...) |
|---|---|---|
| spire | spruce, fir | sapling 2.4 m, young 7, mature 17, old 30 |
| pine | pine | sapling 3.5, pole 10, mature 19, old 26 |
| column | cedar, cypress | 3, 8, 14 |
| round | maple, oak, lime | 3.3, 7, 14, 20 |
| oval | poplar, birch | 3.8, 10, 18, 25 |
| spread | old oak, acacia, olive stand-in | 3.1, 5.3, 10, 14 |
| feather | coconut palm | seedling, young (no trunk), trunked, mature 17 |
| fan | Washingtonia | seedling, young (no trunk), trunked, mature 18 (48 / 144 / 308 / 332 triangles) |

**Drawing (in treelab.html; the app will need its own version):**
- **Normalise at load:** divide each shape by its own height `H = max y` and
  width `W = 2 * max(|x|, |z|)`, so every shape is unit size. One shader then
  stretches it to each tree's real height and width.
- **One tree = 20 bytes:** float32 x, y, z; uint16 height (cm), uint16 width (cm);
  uint8 facing (0-255 = full turn), shade, warmth, shape number.
- **Shader:** `p = aPos * vec3(w, h, w)`, turned by facing; colour
  `* (0.8 + 0.4 * shade)`, mixed toward warm by warmth; shrinks to nothing over
  the last 8% of the range, so trees grow in at the edge rather than pop.
  Same shading as the app's mesh shader.
- **Bins:** one bin per detailed shape (used within "detailed" distance, 200 m)
  plus one per style for the far shape. One instanced draw call per bin: about
  30 calls whatever the number of trees.
- **Refresh:** trees are kept by 100 m cell. The draw list is rebuilt only
  after 20 m of travel or a turn of about 7 degrees: cells in range (and in
  view) are taken nearest first, so the card skips hidden pixels; trees are
  drawn before the ground for the same reason. Standing still costs nothing.
- **Size per tree:** age picks the stage; height and width then vary up to 10%
  either way. Vine pieces keep their exact size and facing, so rows join.

**Measured on Jim's card** (treelab, 300,000 trees): 1.3 million triangles a
frame in 1.85 ms of card time, 60 fps; list refresh 5 to 6 ms of main-program
time. The refresh is the first thing to suffer at larger counts.

**Planting rules that Jim approved (treelab `mountain()`):**
- Forest where the forest outline (here a noise field) says, thinning to a
  treeline with younger, smaller trees near it; none on very steep ground.
- Trees bunch up and thin out, with small clearings, not even spacing.
- Low down: broadleaf in the valley, changing to conifers between about 430 and
  730 m (spire 58%, pine 30%, column 12% in the mix).
- Up the slopes: spruce and fir fill the ground; pines only in their own stands
  (patches 300 to 400 m across) on the sun-facing side, about 30% narrower
  there, with the odd stray (4%) outside.

**Tuscany rules (Jim liked the scene):** vine rows 2.6 m apart; cypresses both
sides of a lane every 9 m, 6 m off its middle, and in small clumps; olive groves
on a 7 m grid (young spreading tree stands in).

### The vine (approved 10 October)

Archetype, not accuracy: whatever reads as a vineyard for the least cost. Jim
dropped trunks, posts and wires: the boxy first try already read as a vineyard.

- **Shape:** 1 m of row standing on the ground. Across the row, one outline:
  narrow at the ground, widest (60 cm across) 1 m up, then a barn roof (two
  slopes each side) to a ridge at 1.7 m. Jim: "slight variations make a huge
  visual impact", so the long lines are never quite level: each end outline
  is moved a few cm, each side on its own.
- **Rows** are chains of row pieces (`library/farm/build.mjs`): three shared
  end outlines, and for each length (10, 3, 1 m) a piece for every start/end
  pair. Each piece must start on the outline the last one ended with; pick the
  next end at random. Use 10 m pieces, then 3 m, then 1 m for what is left.
  Inner metres of long pieces have their own random outlines. A flat cap
  (`vine-cap-<n>`) closes each end; caps are not drawn far away.
- **Cost:** 12 triangles a metre near; far, one level piece per length
  (8 triangles however long). About 122 triangles per 10 m near and 10 far,
  against 240 and 100 for the first boxy vine; and a tenth as many pieces.
- **Slopes:** each piece sits on the ground at its middle and is **sheared**
  (not turned) to the slope along it, so the vines stay upright. In both labs
  the shader reads the slope from the byte trees use for warmth, for row-piece
  draw calls only (`uShear`). One sink depth per row (from the cross slope at
  its middle), or neighbouring pieces show a step.
- **Rows snap to the 256 facings**, so every piece of a row lines up exactly.
- **One shade per row**, not per piece, or joins show a colour step.
- **Stress test** (Tree Stand, Champagne and Niagara), approved by Jim ("seems
  to work perfectly"): Champagne, 2 km of slope, parcels about 90 x 110 m,
  rows 1.2 m apart down the slope, about 2,400 km of row and 360,000 pieces;
  Niagara, 3 km of flat farms, rows 2.7 m apart, about 2,500 km of row.
  The planting code (`plantRow`, `fillBlock` in `lab/stand/template.html`;
  `vineRow` in `treelab.html`) is the model for the app's.

## 6. What the app needs to take it in

1. **Data.** Trees and playground pieces come from OpenStreetMap tags the
   streamed tiles probably do not carry: `natural=tree` (with `leaf_type`,
   `genus`, `species`, `height`), forest outlines (`landuse=forest`,
   `natural=wood`, with `leaf_type`), `landuse=vineyard`, `landuse=orchard` with
   `trees=oil_palms/coconut_palms/date_palms/olive_trees`, `playground=*`.
   `library/tags.json` lists every tag met and how it would be drawn.
   Check what the tiles carry first. Overpass is busy and shared; use it gently
   or not at all. Forests are outlines with no trees in them: plant per tile,
   seeded from the tile, so the same trees come back every visit.
2. **Style from tags:** `leaf_type=needleleaved` -> spire (mostly), pine,
   column; `broadleaved` -> round, oval, spread; genus/species where mapped;
   otherwise a regional default. Palms only in warm places.
3. **Placement on the ball.** The app places each patch per tile with a float64
   origin (`globe.js place`); trees should be per tile too, positions relative
   to the tile's origin, ground height from the ground actually drawn (the
   "one ground" work matters here, or trees float or sink like the torn roads).
4. **Instancing in the mesh program.** `meshprogram.js` draws plain meshes; it
   will need an instanced path (the attributes and shader lines above).
5. **Background refresh.** Build each tile's tree list once, in the worker, as
   the tile arrives; the per-frame work is then choosing tiles, not trees.
6. **Not built yet:** thinning distant trees (draw one in four, enlarged, past
   about 800 m), which would cut far cost by about three quarters.
7. **Vineyards:** the map gives an outline only. Fill it with rows (1.2 to
   1.5 m apart in old French regions, 2.5 to 3 m elsewhere), running down the
   slope, chained and sheared as in "The vine" above. The instanced path needs
   the shear (one per-draw flag and the slope in the warmth byte, or a 24-byte
   instance if warmth is wanted on vines too).
8. **Playground pieces** need a facing: the map gives a point and a type only.
   Face the middle of the playground; where only the playground outline is
   mapped, scatter a few standard pieces inside it, seeded.
